\chapter*{\myAbstractTitle}

An identity provider (IdP) authenticates the users of an organisation across multiple service 
providers (SPs), so that a single misconfigured or compromised IdP can affect every service its 
users access. 
Existing monitoring approaches for IdPs focus on availability rather than on security. 
This thesis proposes a security monitoring system that observes the Shibboleth IdP from within its 
application layer. 
It combines three data sources from the IdP: the audit log, two interceptors that run 
inside the authentication flow and the configuration and metadata of the SPs. 
The records are pseudonymised at their source, sent to a separate backend, correlated with each 
other and checked against fifteen detection rules. 
Thirteen of these rules can be traced to documented vulnerabilities and security recommendations. 

The evaluation shows that the records of a login can be linked reliably. 
Where the audit log records the identifier of the request, every record was linked to the correct 
login. 
Without this identifier, a fallback based on the pseudonymised user and address also linked every 
record correctly in all tested scenarios. 
All rules behaved as specified in tests against a real database, and eleven of them also detected 
their condition in tests of the complete pipeline. 
Legitimate logins raised no false alarms. 
With asynchronous transmission, the interceptors added no measurable login delay but cost about 
28\,ms of processor time per login.

The work also shows the limitations of monitoring an IdP from within. 
Attacks that take place at the SP, in the network or with a stolen signing key remain invisible to 
the system, and an attacker who controls the IdP can suppress the records about their own actions.
