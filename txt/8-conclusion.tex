\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

This thesis presented an application layer monitoring tool for the Shibboleth IdP that detects 
documented vulnerabilities, configuration weaknesses and anomalous authentication behaviour by 
combining three separate sources of information from within the IdP. 
The collected data is transmitted to an external backend, where it is stored, correlated and 
evaluated against a catalogue of fifteen detection rules.

The three sources complemented one another. 
The audit log records the outcome of a completed transaction, the configuration states what was 
intended for the partner involved, and the interceptors observe the incoming request and the 
outgoing response while the transaction is being processed. 
What each source can see follows from its position in the processing sequence, which is why their 
combination allows comparisons that no single source supports on its own. 

The detection rules are derived rather than assembled. 
Thirteen of the fifteen rules can be traced to vulnerabilities and attack classes documented in the 
literature. 
The two rules that cannot be traced are named as such. 
All fifteen are verified against a real Elasticsearch instance, and thirteen additionally through 
the complete pipeline, eleven of them with both a true positive and a true negative test. 

The correlation links the records of a login reliably. 
All 7{,}236 reports of the performance series found their counterpart, and with the identifier of 
the request, or with the subject and the address as long as one of them separates the users, the 
correct one was found in every tested scenario. 
Only the strategy based on time alone (\texttt{TIME\_\allowbreak{}WINDOW}) confused logins, and 
only those that followed each other at the same SP within less than 100\,ms. 

In the asynchronous transmission mode, the two interceptors introduced no measurable latency overhead. 
The difference to a deployment without interceptors is $+6.2$\,ms with an upper bound of 17\,ms for 
the 95\% confidence interval, and the work performed inside the interceptors amounts to about 
0.13\% of the login duration. 
Their cost appears as processor time instead, about 28\,ms per login, while blocking transmission 
would add about 60\,ms to each login without saving any of it. 

The comparison of two independently produced records of the same event serves as a check of log 
integrity without changing the logging chain. 
For 200 legitimate logins the consistency check between outbound report and audit entry raised 
no alert, and it reported exactly the modified field of a deliberately manipulated audit entry. 
The check shows that two records disagree, but not which of them was altered, and it cannot see the 
fields inside an encrypted assertion.

The study also identified limitations that follow from the observation point rather than from the 
effort invested. 
Several federation level attack scenarios occur at a different system layer, involve a different 
party or are observable only through their preconditions. 
One case is more fundamental, since a malicious IdP cannot be detected by a system 
running inside it, where observer and adversary coincide. 
The severity classification makes a further boundary explicit, as none of the fifteen rules 
reaches the highest risk level, which presupposes a compromise of the signing key. 

What this thesis contributes is therefore not a new architecture for collecting and correlating 
security events. 
It is the demonstration that an IdP holds security relevant information which no 
log consuming system can obtain, an analysis of which properties are observable from which 
position and an implementation that makes this information available at a cost that can be 
stated in numbers rather than assumed.
